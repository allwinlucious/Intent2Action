# Learning tasks from human video demonstrations for robots

## 1. Search log

Search date: **2026-10-31**. Target window: **2026-04-01 through 2026-10-31**. Citation counts are Semantic Scholar counts retrieved on the search date; GitHub stars and Hugging Face (HF) paper upvotes are snapshots from the same date. No 2025 paper was needed.

- **arXiv API.** I ran six broad queries (`human video demonstration robot imitation`, `video task specification robot`, `video to program robot manipulation`, `task intent human demonstration robot`, `goal inference video robot manipulation`, and `learning from observation robot video`), each with `max_results=100` and descending submission date. They returned **600 records / 296 unique records in the date window** after deduplication, but broad `all:` matching produced heavy keyword noise. I then ran eight narrower formulations combining: `"human video"`, `"task intent"`, `"goal inference"`, `"learning/imitation from observation"`, `"video-to-program"`, `"video demonstration"`, `"task planning"`, `"task specification"`, `generalization`, and `robot`, with the date constraint embedded in the query.
- **Semantic Scholar.** I queried `human video robot imitation`, `robot learn task from human video`, `video task specification robot`, `video program robot manipulation`, `one-shot human demonstration robot`, and `imitation from observation robot`, then looked up finalists by arXiv ID. The search endpoint was rate-limited on five of six broad requests; the successful request returned **100 of 318** results. Of those, **37** were in-window matches to the topic terms. Individual-ID lookups supplied citation and influential-citation counts where available.
- **Primary-source checks.** I read arXiv abstracts/source, project pages, released datasets, and GitHub repositories for **13 finalists**. I also checked HF paper pages for community attention. A paper was kept only when at least two credibility signals survived inspection (venue, citations, established group, released artifact, substantial real-robot/large-scale evaluation, or community attention).
- **Outcome.** **8 papers kept**, ranked below; **5 representative rejects** are documented. The ranking weights evidence quality and relevance to *task intent/specification and new-layout execution*, not just visual similarity or hand-trajectory retargeting.

## 2. Kept papers

### 1. Zero-WAM: In-Context World-Action Modeling from Human Videos for Open-Ended Task Generalization

- **Paper:** [arXiv:2608.26103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26103), submitted **2026-08-26**. Authors are from **Robbyant, HKUST (Guangzhou), and HKUST** ([project](https://robbyant-research.github.io/Zero-WAM/)).
- **Credibility signals:** arXiv preprint; top-venue acceptance **unknown**. **22 citations / 0 influential citations** ([Semantic Scholar API](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/arXiv:2608.26103?fields=title,citationCount,influentialCitationCount,venue)). [Code](https://github.com/robbyant-research/Zero-WAM), data, and pre-/post-trained weights are released; **445 GitHub stars**. **25 HF upvotes**. Evaluation uses 43 training and 7 held-out RoboTwin tasks, **100 closed-loop rollouts per task over 3 seeds**, plus a real bimanual Franka evaluation.
- **What they did:** Treat a human demonstration video as an in-context task specification rather than merely a source of motion. A causal world-action model predicts future robot video and actions from that prompt. Their HumanGen pipeline converts task-sampled robot trajectories into **74.2K semantically matched human-robot pairs over 8.6K tasks**; an in-context future-chunk prediction (IFP) objective forces the model to use the video instead of shortcutting through text or robot history.
- **Key quantitative results:** **46.95%** mean success on seven unseen RoboTwin tasks versus **17.45%** for LingBot-VA and **10.98%** for WAN-Action. IFP raises the held-out-task average from **28.55% to 46.95%**. On real unseen configurations, Zero-WAM gets **53.3%** object-to-container, **33.3%** ordered three-object manipulation, and **16.7%** two-leg insertion, versus **43.3%, 10.0%, and 0.0%** for the language-conditioned baseline.
- **Borrow for our project:**
  1. Add an auxiliary training target that predicts the *future symbolic world-state chunks implied by the demonstration* from the inferred program/specification. This is the program analogue of IFP and directly tests whether the VLM used the video rather than object priors.
  2. Build paired training data by taking successful simulator programs, rendering their state evolution as human-style videos, and retaining pairs only after semantic and physical consistency checks. Use task-balanced sampling so common pick-and-place tasks do not swamp rare rules.
  3. Copy the evaluation split: hold out entire rules/tasks, not just layouts, and report both task-level zero-shot success and configuration-level generalization with multiple seeds.
- **Difference from our project:** Zero-WAM remains a large video-action model in the control loop; our system compiles the demonstration into readable Python and normally executes it through deterministic robot primitives with no model in the loop.

### 2. Dex-One2Many: Learning Dexterous Manipulation from a Single Human Demonstration

- **Paper:** [arXiv:2610.12470](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12470), submitted **2026-10-08**. Authors are from **Seoul National University, University of Maryland, KAIST, and All Purpose AI** ([project](https://dex-one2many.github.io/)).
- **Credibility signals:** arXiv preprint; top-venue acceptance **unknown**. **0 citations / 0 influential citations** ([Semantic Scholar API](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/arXiv:2610.12470?fields=title,citationCount,influentialCitationCount,venue)); **2 HF upvotes**. Code release **unknown**. Strong evaluation: five tasks, **30 simulation episodes and 30 real trials per method/task** under a seen/unseen protocol; a second simulation study evaluates **500 episodes per task across four arm-hand embodiments**. Real UR3 + 20-DoF hand experiments and component ablations are included.
- **What they did:** This is the clearest recent example of extracting the *task rather than the trajectory*. One VLM call turns the complete video into an ordered, stage-wise scene graph over a fixed predicate vocabulary such as `pre_grasp`, `grasp`, `contact`, and `inside`. The graph deliberately discards coordinates and motion paths; its predicates generate diverse stage reset states and dense RL rewards, allowing the robot to discover different poses, grasps, and paths that satisfy the same task structure.
- **Key quantitative results:** On unseen initial and goal configurations, Dex-One2Many reaches **85–100%** simulation success and **60–85%** real-robot success, while baselines remain **≤15%**; the reported real unseen-config mean is **75%**, **71 percentage points** above the next best baseline. Across four simulated embodiments, mean success is **94.6–96.8%** over five tasks. Removing stage-wise resets drops success after 2K PPO updates from over **90% to about 16%**; removing predicate-derived rewards leaves success at **0%**.
- **Borrow for our project:**
  1. Make the video-to-intent output an explicit typed graph before emitting Python: entities and functional parts as nodes; predicates and temporal stage boundaries as edges. Compile that graph into `find`/`pick`/`place`/`verify` calls. This gives the user and runtime an inspectable contract.
  2. Use a small, task-independent predicate vocabulary with executable semantics. Each predicate should define both a runtime verifier and a simulator data generator; e.g. `inside(a,b)` supplies a placement constraint, a postcondition, and randomized training layouts.
  3. Reproduce its **seen pose vs unseen initial/goal pose** protocol, then add our own unseen-rule and recovery splits. Ablate graph staging, functional-part grounding, and verification separately.
- **Difference from our project:** Dex-One2Many trains a task-specific RL policy in simulation from the graph; our graph would be compiled into a short symbolic program that calls an existing motion/grasp stack.

### 3. HOST: Robots Acquire Manipulation Skills in Seconds from a Single Human Video

- **Paper:** [arXiv:2607.20033](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20033), submitted **2026-07-22**. Authors are from **Beijing Institute of Technology, X SQUARE ROBOT, and Tsinghua University** ([project](https://host-site.host-robotics.workers.dev/)).
- **Credibility signals:** arXiv preprint; top-venue acceptance **unknown**. **10 citations / 1 influential citation** ([Semantic Scholar API](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/arXiv:2607.20033?fields=title,citationCount,influentialCitationCount,venue)). [Code](https://github.com/CGuangyan-BIT/HOST) released, **144 GitHub stars**; **4 HF upvotes**. Physical bimanual platform evaluation spans **50 novel tasks × 20 randomized trials = 1,000 trials**, backed by 193,462 robot trajectories over 229 tasks and 5,847 human videos.
- **What they did:** HOST handles asynchronous human and robot execution by learning a shared task-progress manifold. At each cycle it localizes the robot within the demonstration, predicts the robot's own future observations for the upcoming demonstrated progress, then derives actions from those predictions. It first pretrains on cheap robot-robot same-task pairs and then adapts on the much smaller human-robot paired set; no per-task parameter update is needed at deployment.
- **Key quantitative results:** **62%** mean success from one human video, **43 points** above the strongest one-shot visual-imitation baseline and **45 points** above the strongest zero-shot language policy. It beats the best LoRA-fine-tuned baseline trained on 50 robot demonstrations (**56%**) while using 50× fewer task demonstrations and acquiring a skill in **29 s**, reported as **507× faster**. Full target coupling improves success from **0.45 to 0.62**; the full localization→future-observation→action cascade improves a direct-action baseline from **0.34 to 0.62**. Under lighting, object, scene, and human-disturbance shifts, losses from the 62% default are 1, 4, 6, and 9 points.
- **Borrow for our project:**
  1. Align each video frame to *task progress*, not clock time, before inferring stages. HOST reduces human-annotated progress error from **0.079 to 0.006** versus timestamp matching; this should make program step boundaries and resume points much more stable.
  2. Train program recovery on paired traces of different executions of the same rule: locate the failed program step, predict the intended next world state, then choose a repair tool call. Pretrain this structure on simulator-simulator pairs before using costly human-video pairs.
  3. Add acquisition latency, recurring-task reuse, and perturbation-specific success (lighting/object/layout/human disturbance) to the benchmark, alongside overall task success.
- **Difference from our project:** HOST is an end-to-end generative video/action policy with a large paired corpus; our output is a persistent Python artifact whose state and control flow are explicit.

### 4. SeeTraceAct: Visibility-Aware Latent Planning from Cross-Embodiment Demonstration Videos

- **Paper:** [arXiv:2606.02745](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02745), submitted **2026-06-01**, **CoRL 2026**. Authors are from **Georgia Tech, Allen Institute for AI, Johns Hopkins, and University of Washington** ([project](https://jaehyeon-son.github.io/seetraceact/)).
- **Credibility signals:** accepted at **CoRL 2026**. **3 citations / 1 influential citation** ([Semantic Scholar API](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/arXiv:2606.02745?fields=title,citationCount,influentialCitationCount,venue)); **1 HF upvote**. [RoboCasa-DC](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Sondroid/RoboCasa-DC) is released with 24 tasks, 2,400 training trajectory pairs, and 50 evaluation seeds/task. The [GitHub repository](https://github.com/jaehyeon-son/SeeTraceAct) has **7 stars**, but contained only a release placeholder at inspection time, so code availability is not counted as a signal. Real Franka experiments cover four unseen tasks with 10 trials/task.
- **What they did:** A one-shot demonstration-conditioned VLA receives the video, current observations, and language. During training, learnable latent-plan tokens must predict future end-effector traces in each camera view plus a validity bit for whether each trace point is visible; the trace decoder is removed at inference. This auxiliary target forces precise spatial grounding, especially when the relevant interaction area is tiny.
- **Key quantitative results:** On five held-out RoboCasa-DC tasks per split, SeeTraceAct is best in all four settings; precision-sensitive cross-embodiment success is **12.8%** versus **8.4%** for the best baseline. The project reports **50.0%** real-world mean versus **37.5%** for the strongest baseline. Removing trace supervision changes cross-embodiment success from **12.2% to 9.4%**, and removing visibility validity drops it to **8.2%**. Gain is inversely correlated with target-interaction size (**Spearman ρ = −0.63, p < 10⁻³**).
- **Borrow for our project:**
  1. Add an auxiliary grounding target while training video-to-program: predict a sparse trace or target region for each emitted API call, plus a typed `visible: bool`/confidence field. This can regularize the program writer without putting the trace model in the execution loop.
  2. Stratify evaluation by **target interaction ratio** or analogous target-region size. Report validity and task success separately for small buttons/holes, medium objects, and large receptacles.
  3. Release paired simulator demonstrations and fixed evaluation seeds so other video-to-specification systems can compare on identical cross-embodiment inputs.
- **Difference from our project:** SeeTraceAct learns latent spatial plans and directly predicts actions; it does not infer a readable rule or generate a robot-API program.

### 5. LUCID: Learning Embodiment-Agnostic Intent Models from Unstructured Human Videos for Scalable Dexterous Robot Skill Acquisition

- **Paper:** [arXiv:2606.11628](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11628), submitted **2026-06-10**. Authors are from **University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign and Carnegie Mellon University** ([project](https://lucid-robot.github.io/)).
- **Credibility signals:** arXiv preprint; top-venue acceptance **unknown**. **7 citations / 0 influential citations** ([Semantic Scholar API](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/arXiv:2606.11628?fields=title,citationCount,influentialCitationCount,venue)). Code, weights, and HF upvotes: **unknown**. Established UIUC/CMU groups and a substantial real-robot study: three web-video tasks × three scenarios × ten trials, plus two smartphone-video tasks × two embodiments × fifteen trials (**150 physical trials total**).
- **What they did:** LUCID explicitly separates robot-agnostic intent from embodiment-specific control. From the live RGB-D observation, the intent model predicts one second of object flow plus a rough palm pose; a task-agnostic policy trained with massively parallel simulation converts this reference to robot action. Intent is re-queried in closed loop, and the same interface drives both a dexterous hand and a parallel-jaw gripper.
- **Key quantitative results:** On stirring, wiping, and binning learned from about **20K internet clips/task**, closed-loop LUCID reaches **73%** success versus **28%** for a Veo 3.1 open-loop video-plan baseline. On push-T and cable routing learned from **1 hour of phone video/task**, both robot embodiments obtain **19/30 (63%)** aggregate success. The paper also shows monotonic improvement in held-out intent loss and real success as binning data scales from 1K to 20K clips.
- **Borrow for our project:**
  1. Preserve the architectural boundary: a robot-independent intent representation feeding a robot-specific executor. For us, replace dense flow with typed relational postconditions and let the existing production stack solve embodiment-specific motion.
  2. Benchmark **re-plan/recover from current state** against a single open-loop program. Use their failure buckets—perception/occlusion, unstable grasp leading to an unrecoverable state, and stale plan after divergence—as the start of a recovery taxonomy.
  3. Plot video-data scale against both intent/specification error and downstream task success; this will reveal whether SFT improves actual programs or only offline labels.
- **Difference from our project:** LUCID learns short-horizon geometric intent from thousands of clips per task and continuously queries it; our target is a one-demonstration, long-horizon symbolic rule compiled once into code.

### 6. MoCaP: Mobile Code-as-Policies for Zero-Shot Mobile Manipulation from Passive Human Videos

- **Paper:** [arXiv:2610.20998](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20998), submitted **2026-10-14**. Authors are from **New York University, NYU Shanghai, and Fudan University** ([project](https://mocap-7.github.io/MoCAP-page/)).
- **Credibility signals:** arXiv preprint; top-venue acceptance **unknown**. **1 citation / 0 influential citations** ([Semantic Scholar API](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/arXiv:2610.20998?fields=title,citationCount,influentialCitationCount,venue)); **0 HF upvotes**. [Code](https://github.com/MoCAP-7/MoCAP) released, **7 GitHub stars**. Real mobile-manipulator experiments include 30 MoCaP manipulation trials, navigation comparisons with 10 episodes/method, and a 90-trial approach-prior comparison. The authors correctly characterize the study as feasibility evidence in one environment, not broad generalization.
- **What they did:** Passive egocentric video is processed offline into reusable spatial memory and human approach priors. At runtime, a VLM code agent emits short Python programs over grounded navigation, perception, grasp, and motion-planning primitives. Failed calls interrupt the rest of the program; diagnostics and a fresh observation return to the agent, which writes the next program while conversation history persists.
- **Key quantitative results:** On a real mobile manipulator, MoCaP succeeds on **16/20 box trials and 8/10 can trials (80% each)**, while CaP-X and its collision-aware navigation variant achieve 0 successes. Video spatial memory improves trash-bin navigation from **7/10 to 10/10**. The human approach prior improves readiness from **18/45 to 38/45** overall and from **0/15 to 14/15** at the most unfavorable start.
- **Borrow for our project:**
  1. Closely copy the execution contract: run short sandboxed programs; stop at the first failed primitive; return the failing call, structured diagnostic, current observation/world state, and retained history; then resume from a repaired program suffix.
  2. Keep motion feasibility inside trusted primitives. MoCaP's useful distinction is that the model chooses *what/where*, while cuRobo, costmaps, IK, swept-volume checks, and grasp filters decide what is executable.
  3. Evaluate recovery by failure stage (search/localization, approach/base pose, IK/reachability, collision, grasp, verification), not only final success; report attempted-grasp and readiness rates as intermediate diagnostics.
- **Difference from our project:** MoCaP's video supplies environment memory and approach cues, not the demonstrated task rule, and its model repeatedly writes programs during normal execution; ours aims for one compiled program and invokes the model only on failure or uncertain semantic checks.

### 7. Real2Sem2Real: 3D Semantic Augmentations for Robot Learning from a Single Human RGB Demonstration

- **Paper:** [arXiv:2610.28880](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28880), submitted **2026-10-20**. Authors are from **Stanford University and ETH Zurich** ([project](https://real2sem2real.github.io/)).
- **Credibility signals:** arXiv preprint; top-venue acceptance **unknown**. **0 citations / 0 influential citations** ([Semantic Scholar API](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/arXiv:2610.28880?fields=title,citationCount,influentialCitationCount,venue)); HF upvotes and code release **unknown**. Strong established groups and unusually large hardware evaluation: four tasks, two robot embodiments, 40–50 trials per task/robot/condition, plus data-scaling studies—**nearly 2,000 real rollouts** reported. The generated-data pipeline produces 2,000 episodes in about two hours on one RTX 5090.
- **What they did:** From one monocular human RGB video, R2Sem2Real tracks object SE(3), retargets the human grasp, and transfers the demonstration to generated “digital cousins.” DINOv3 feature alignments match both whole objects and functionally important parts such as a mug handle or coffee-machine tray. It generates category-diverse robot demonstrations in Isaac Sim or MuJoCo and fine-tunes a π0.5 VLA for either xArm7 or Franka.
- **Key quantitative results:** Semantic augmentation beats the same pipeline restricted to the demonstrated objects by **26–54 points** on every task/robot and matches or exceeds a policy trained on **150 teleoperated demonstrations**. On xArm7 coffee-machine placement, Isaac Sim data yields **45/50 (90%)** versus **31/50 (62%)** from MuJoCo and 62% from teleoperation. Single-pair policies plateau at 10–12% (teleoperation) or 36–38% (generated), while semantically varied data continues improving.
- **Borrow for our project:**
  1. Ground task predicates to **functional parts**, not only category labels: `grasp(handle(mug))`, `place(bottom(mug), tray(machine))`, `insert(peg, selected_hole)`. Use feature similarity to transfer those roles to unseen instances.
  2. Generate program-training and evaluation worlds by swapping category-consistent “digital cousins” in Isaac Sim. Filter samples on IK, collision, settling, and postcondition verification exactly as the runtime would.
  3. Compare spatial-only augmentation with semantic-instance augmentation; report success separately on seen objects, held-out instances, and held-out pairings of source and target categories.
- **Difference from our project:** R2Sem2Real turns one video into thousands of trajectories to train a continuous VLA; our system infers a compact rule/program and relies on general robot primitives at execution.

### 8. Video2Sim2Real: Full-Stack Autonomous Dexterous Skill Acquisition from a Single Human Video

- **Paper:** [arXiv:2606.08828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08828), submitted **2026-06-07**. Authors are from **Georgia Tech, Toyota Research Institute, University of Pennsylvania, and Carnegie Mellon University** ([project/code](https://github.com/video2sim2real/video2sim2real)).
- **Credibility signals:** arXiv preprint; top-venue acceptance **unknown**. **9 citations / 0 influential citations** ([Semantic Scholar API](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/arXiv:2606.08828?fields=title,citationCount,influentialCitationCount,venue)); **0 HF upvotes**. Code released, **24 GitHub stars**. Established multi-institution group; seven real tasks × ten trials and extensive three-seed simulation baselines/ablations on a Kinova Gen3 + LEAP hand.
- **What they did:** The system reconstructs a simulator-ready digital twin from one human video, retargets hand/object motion, and then corrects the unreliable trajectory only at sparse object-centric keyframes. Those optimized robot configurations become anchors for smooth interpolation. A distilled arm policy plus residual finger RL handles sim-to-real variation, while collision-aware motion planning relocates the anchors for new object configurations.
- **Key quantitative results:** Sparse keyframe refinement averages **91.4%** success across seven simulation tasks; the next-best aggregate baseline is **52.9%**. In real object-pose variations, the full system succeeds on **67/70 trials (95.7%)**, versus **11/70 (15.7%)** for the FoundationPose adjustment baseline. The study also reports success, safety, arm/hand RMS jerk, and completion time, with ten simulation runs/task and three seeds for learned baselines.
- **Borrow for our project:**
  1. When a rule alone is insufficient for delicate contact, extract only sparse object-centric keyframes as optional arguments or constraints to a trusted primitive—never treat the full human hand path as ground truth.
  2. Use the same anchors to synthesize new-layout executions through the motion planner, producing stack-specific SFT/recovery data without teaching the model low-level kinematics.
  3. Add **safety rate, planner feasibility, and completion/repair time** to task success; for continuous motions, RMS jerk is useful, while for our discrete programs the analogous metric is unnecessary/redundant primitive calls.
- **Difference from our project:** Video2Sim2Real is still trajectory/keyframe-centric and trains task policies; our central representation is a semantic rule compiled to code, with planners responsible for all motion.

## 3. Rejected examples

- **[HumanEgo: Zero-Shot Robot Learning from Minutes of Human Egocentric Videos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24934):** rejected despite 23 citations and high attention because the arXiv source explicitly says its main real-world, auxiliary-training, and generalization experiments are “still in progress” and that many plotted values are placeholders; the headline numbers therefore fail the credibility filter.
- **[ActiveGlasses: Learning Manipulation with Active Vision from Ego-centric Human Demonstration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08534):** credible real-robot active-vision work, but it transfers extracted object trajectories and head motion; it does not infer task goals/rules or a reusable task specification.
- **[One Demonstration, Many Objects: Generalizing Manipulation via Local Contact Geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01938):** strong Stanford real-robot study (71% over 16 objects, four tasks, two hands), but it learns local contact/motion imitation rather than the intended task relation or plan, making it less relevant than the eight retained papers.
- **[VidAct: Learning Manipulation from In-the-Wild Videos with Object-Centric 3D Awareness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36870):** reconstructs and transfers object trajectories and adds privileged point-cloud prediction; the abstract gives no numerical evaluation scale/result, and the representation remains motion-shaped rather than rule/task-specification shaped.
- **[RGraph-Eval: Task-Grounded Scene Graphs for Evaluating Robot Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31121):** its temporal scene graph is interesting, but the paper evaluates generated videos and human-judgment agreement on 1,000 scenes; it contains no learned robot execution or task success experiment.

## 4. Takeaways

- **Use a typed, stage-wise relational specification as the compiler IR.** Dex-One2Many is the closest prior art on extracting a task rule rather than a trajectory: entities/functional parts, predicates, and their order. The project's novelty should not claim to be the first to abstract one video into relational task structure; it can claim the distinct step of compiling that structure into readable, sandboxed robot-API Python with runtime postconditions.
- **Prevent “video ignoring” during training.** Zero-WAM's future-chunk objective and HOST's progress-coupled targets show that simply providing video tokens is not enough. Train the program writer to predict future symbolic world states and align program steps to learned task progress; ablate both. Evaluate entire held-out rules as well as unseen layouts.
- **Make functional parts and uncertainty first-class.** Real2Sem2Real shows that category-level transfer depends on handles, rims, trays, holes, and other task-specific regions. SeeTraceAct shows the value of a separate validity/visibility head. Represent semantic decisions as calibrated typed values (`label`, `confidence`, `visible`, `evidence`) and ask the user below a threshold; report calibration error, risk-coverage, and task success under abstention.
- **Keep embodiment and feasibility below the intent/program layer.** LUCID's intent/controller split, Video2Sim2Real's sparse anchors, and MoCaP's checked primitives all support the project's architecture: the model says what relation should become true; the planner, grasp system, collision checker, and verifier decide how and whether it can be achieved. Generate fine-tuning data from those exact stack diagnostics.
- **State the overlap honestly, then emphasize the combination.** Zero-WAM/HOST overlap on one-video task conditioning; Dex-One2Many overlaps on relational intent; MoCaP overlaps closely on short Python programs, trusted primitives, first-failure interruption, diagnostics, and resumption. None of the kept papers combines **one phone video → explicit rule → readable sandboxed program → model-free normal execution → model-on-failure repair → calibrated semantic guards → fine-tuning on the production stack's own feedback**. That combined systems claim, backed by program validity, unseen-layout/task success, repair success by failure class, and calibration metrics, is the defensible novelty.
